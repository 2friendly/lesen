## Design Context

### Users
Children in pre-school, kindergarten, and Years 1–3 who are learning to decode words and read independently. They use the app for short, focused reading exercises, often on a touch device and potentially alongside a parent or teacher.

### Brand Personality
Calm, playful, and confidence-building. The product should feel friendly without becoming visually noisy or babyish.

The product name is **lesen**, the German verb “to read.” The primary wordmark may be paired with its IPA pronunciation, `/ˈleːzn̩/`, as a quiet secondary detail.

### Aesthetic Direction
A warm, light, storybook-inspired interface with extremely simple navigation. Reading screens remove nearly everything except one sentence and the current action. Large, plain letterforms and generous touch targets take priority over decorative styling.

### Design Principles

- Keep each reading screen to one sentence and one obvious action.
- Make the reading type simpler than the surrounding brand typography.
- Prefer natural word shapes and spacing so children see familiar written language.
- Give immediate, gentle feedback without rushing or distracting the child.
- Require playful elements to reinforce sound, meaning, or reading progress.
- Support touch, keyboard navigation, reduced motion, and browser zoom from the start.
